# First Look

Your comp card, portfolio and rate card from one set of facts, checked against what an agency, a promoter or a
client asks of someone new.

A first look is what they see before they meet you. This is a free tool for new models, DJs and musicians, dancers
and actors, photographers and other creatives. Put your facts in once, and it makes:

- a **comp card**, front and back, at A5, 5.5 x 8.5 in or 4 x 6 in, 300 DPI, with 3 mm bleed and crop marks for a
  print shop
- a **portfolio**: a cover, then one piece a page, never cropped
- a **rate card** that never leaves without its terms
- a **digitals sheet** for models, a **one sheet**, and a block of plain text for agency forms

Then tell it who you are sending to. It knows what each side asks for first from someone new: four plain digitals
for a model agency, one mix a promoter can play on a phone, a headshot and a showreel for casting. It checks what
you have against that, shows the source for every rule, and puts it at the front of one file, with your comp card,
portfolio and rate card behind it.

Four layouts, six sets of type, six colour sets or your own. Nothing you export carries anyone else's branding.

## What it does not do

- It uploads nothing. Your facts, measurements and photographs stay in your browser. Photographs you export are
  redrawn, so any location your phone wrote into them is gone.
- It asks for no account and no email.
- It never decides your rate. It does insist that a rate leaves with its terms.
- It checks the shape of what you say, not whether it is true. Every fact is yours to stand behind.
- It does not find you an agency. A real agency never charges you to sign you or to photograph you.

## Run it

Download `index.html` and open it in a browser. It works offline. From a web address, such as this repository's
GitHub Pages copy, it can be installed as an app: on a phone, Share, then "Add to Home Screen".

No build step, no dependencies, no packages. One HTML file.

Photographs are kept in the browser's own storage (IndexedDB). Opened straight from disk, some browsers refuse that
storage; the tool still works, says so, and Save to a file keeps everything, photographs included.
